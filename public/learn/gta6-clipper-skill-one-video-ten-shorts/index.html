Give it any link — a GTA 6 stream, a podcast, a long gameplay video. My GTA 6 Clipper skill finds the 10 loudest reaction moments, builds the 65/35 split layout, syncs MrBeast-style captions word by word, and posts the best 1–2. Here is the full pipeline, step by step.

Give it any link — a GTA 6 stream, a podcast, a long gameplay video — and one skill does the rest. This is GTA 6 Clipper: a skill I built that takes one long creator video (iShowSpeed, Techno Gamerz, CarryMinati, or any facecam gameplay video) and turns it into vertical shorts.

# GTA 6 Clipper

Input: one long video file or YouTube link, creator name + credit handle, caption language (standing choice: English captions). Output: up to 10 vertical shorts; post the best 1–2 to Instagram + YouTube — posting needs approval first; YouTube upload via Studio.

## Step 1 — Find 10 moments (STOP for approval)
- Download with yt-dlp at best quality ≤1080p into the project folder.
- Transcribe the full video with faster-whisper, word timestamps on.
- Score moments on REACTION + CAUSE: find loudness peaks (voice jumps ~6 dB+ over normal, shouting/laughing/screaming, sudden silence, face leaning in) from an RMS/loudness curve; go back 2–5 s in the gameplay for the cause (crash, kill, reveal, fail). A great clip has cause → reaction.
- Each clip 25–55 s, starts on the hook (first 1 s grabs), ends on the punchline, never fades out.
- Present a table: # | start | end | hook line | why viral | cuts (planned switches). Wait for approval before rendering.

## Step 2 — Layout (1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264, AAC)
- TOP 65% (y 0–1248): gameplay, cropped around the action, full width, never a black bar.
- BOTTOM 35% (y 1248–1920): creator face — crop the webcam box, scale to fill; whole face, both eyes, mouth and hands in frame; follow the face smoothly if they move; never cut off the head.
- No facecam → gameplay full screen over a blurred copy of itself darkened 30%.
- Thin 4 px white line on the join at y 1248.

## Step 3 — Smart A-roll / B-roll cuts
- Views: SPLIT (default 65/35), B-ROLL FULL (gameplay fills 9:16; blurred copy behind if needed), A-ROLL FULL (face fills 9:16, head-and-shoulders zoom).
- Around every big moment the order is: SPLIT → B-ROLL FULL (cause, 1–3 s) → A-ROLL FULL (reaction peak, 1–2.5 s, cut in 0.1 s before the peak) → SPLIT.
- Never a view shorter than 0.8 s, max 4 switches per 30 s, never cut mid-word. Add a 6% zoom pop on each cut. No-facecam clips: 10% zoom punch on the reaction instead of A-ROLL FULL.

## Step 4 — MrBeast captions (word-synced, hard-burned)
- From the word timestamps; spot-check 3 words against the audio, fix any >0.05 s off.
- 1–3 words at a time, ALL CAPS, at the join (y ≈ 1080–1230), Komika Axis (~110 px), white fill, 12 px black outline, 6 px soft shadow, current word #FFE600, hype word (WOW/NO WAY/WHAT) #2BFF5A, pop 70% → 110% → 100% in 0.12 s. Safe x 130–950, never over the face's mouth.

## Step 5 — Polish
- Game audio −12 dB under the voice, no music, no watermark, 8% face zoom punch on the peak, creator credit line (~y 320) in the first 3 s.

## Step 6 — Output
- First clip only: export 3 frames (split, b-roll full, a-roll full) for approval before rendering all 10.
- Clips: clips/[creator]-gta6-01..10.mp4, each with a hook-title + hashtag .txt. Post the best 1–2 to Instagram + YouTube (needs approval first).

Step 1 — Find the 10 moments

Nothing gets rendered before the moments are approved. This step is only about finding them.

  • Download the video with yt-dlp at best quality up to 1080p.
  • Transcribe the full video with faster-whisper, word timestamps on.
  • Score moments on reaction + cause: find loudness peaks from an RMS/loudness curve — voice jumps of ~6 dB or more over normal, shouting, laughing, screaming, sudden silence, the face leaning in. Then go back 2–5 seconds in the gameplay for the cause: the crash, the kill, the reveal, the fail. A great clip has cause → reaction.
  • Each clip runs 25–55 seconds, starts on the hook (the first 1 second has to grab), ends on the punchline, and never fades out.
  • The skill presents a table — #, start, end, hook line, why viral, planned cuts — and waits for my OK before rendering anything.

Step 2 — The 65/35 layout

Every short is 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 with AAC audio.

  • Top 65% (y 0–1248): gameplay, cropped around the action, full width, never a black bar.
  • Bottom 35% (y 1248–1920): the creator's face — crop the webcam box and scale to fill. Whole face, both eyes, mouth and hands in frame; follow the face smoothly if they move; never cut off the head.
  • No facecam? Gameplay goes full screen over a blurred copy of itself, darkened 30%.
  • A thin 4 px white line sits on the join at y 1248.

Step 3 — Smart A-roll / B-roll cuts

A static split gets boring fast, so the skill cuts between three views:

  • SPLIT — the default 65/35 layout.
  • B-ROLL FULL — gameplay fills the whole 9:16 (blurred copy behind if needed).
  • A-ROLL FULL — the face fills 9:16, head-and-shoulders zoom.

Around every big moment the order is fixed: SPLIT → B-ROLL FULL (the cause, 1–3 s) → A-ROLL FULL (the reaction peak, 1–2.5 s, cut in 0.1 s before the peak, found from audio level + face change) → SPLIT.

Rules that keep it tight: no view under 0.8 s, max 4 full-screen switches per 30 s, hard cuts only, a 6% zoom pop on A-ROLL FULL, no fades. And if there is no facecam, A-ROLL FULL is skipped — B-ROLL FULL gets a 10% zoom punch on the peak instead.

Step 4 — MrBeast-style captions, perfectly synced

Captions are where most auto-clippers fall apart. This skill is strict:

  • Word-level timings from the transcript (Whisper / forced alignment — never sentence-level). Three random words per clip get spot-checked; anything more than 0.05 s off gets fixed.
  • 1–3 words at a time, ALL CAPS, centred, just above the gameplay/face join (y ≈ 1080–1230) over the gameplay — never over the face. In A-ROLL FULL they sit under the chin, never on eyes or mouth.
  • Font: Komika Axis (fallback The Bold Font / Montserrat Black), ~110 px, white fill, 12 px black outline, hard shadow 6 px down in black.
  • The current word is yellow (#FFE600); one hype word per line — money, numbers, GTA, names — is green (#2BFF5A).
  • Each group pops in 70% → 110% → 100% over 0.12 s. No fade-outs. Text stays inside x 130–950, the platform-UI safe zone.

Step 5 — Polish

  • Keep the original voice; game audio sits −12 dB under speech.
  • Smaller reactions that don't earn an A-ROLL FULL still get an 8% face zoom punch for a moment.
  • A small "credit: @handle" line at the top of the gameplay (~y 320).
  • No background music, no watermark, no emojis over the face.

Step 6 — Output

  • First, 3 frames from clip 1 — SPLIT, B-ROLL FULL, A-ROLL FULL, all with captions — for my OK. All clips render only after I say yes.
  • Files save as clips/[creator]-gta6-01.mp4 through -10.mp4, each with a .txt holding a hook title + 5 hashtags.
  • The best 1–2 get posted: Instagram @cod.ehx and YouTube Untold Hx.

Why this works

The whole skill is built on one idea: cause → reaction. The loudness curve finds where the creator loses it; the 2–5 second look-back finds what caused it; the cut order (gameplay full-screen for the cause, face full-screen for the peak) lets the viewer feel both in under three seconds. Everything else — the 65/35 split, the word-synced captions, the safe zones — is just making sure nothing distracts from that.

One long video in. Ten clips out. One skill.
