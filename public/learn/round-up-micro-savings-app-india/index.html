Someone Please Build This in India: The Round-Up Micro-Savings App

One idea from my first business-idea reel: an app that rounds up every UPI payment to the next round number and saves the spare change. Here is the full roadmap — the idea, who it is for, how to build it, what it costs, and the exact launch steps.

First, the correction

This reel had one business idea in it — not several. The truck-parking idea was a different reel; it is not in this post, and it gets its own roadmap if it gets one.

This is my first business-idea reel, posted 17 November. The idea is a Round-Up Micro-Savings App for India — an app that rounds up every payment to the next round number and automatically saves (or invests) the spare change.

One line before the roadmap, because it matters: this idea was pitched in the reel; we did not build or launch this business ourselves. The roadmap below is the plan to build it — not a story about having done it.

The idea, as pitched in the reel

  • Hook: "Guys, someone please build this in India."
  • Premise: "An app that saves money for you every time you spend money."
  • Example 1: Coffee ₹180 → ₹200, ₹20 saved.
  • Example 2: Food order ₹470 → ₹500, ₹30 saved.
  • Precedent: The UK app Moneybox — 1 million+ users, the same model already running there.
  • Punchline: "I don't have a spending problem. I have a saving problem."
  • Ending poll: YES — round up everything / NO — I like being broke.

Who it is for

UPI users aged 22–35 — students, first-job salaried people, freelancers — who make 3–5 small payments a day on Swiggy, Zomato, Zara, coffee and cabs, but can't set aside a big amount at month-end.

The problem it solves

People promise themselves they'll save, and spending breaks the promise in three days. The problem isn't spending — it's friction in saving. Taking out a big amount at once hurts; ₹10–₹30 doesn't feel like anything. At an average of ₹20 × 3 payments a day, that's ₹1,800 a month saved without feeling it.

How to build it, step by step

  1. See the transactions. An app can't intercept a UPI payment directly. The right route in India is an Account Aggregator (a provider like Finvu or Setu) reading the bank statement with the user's permission. In Phase 1, have the user upload a statement PDF or screenshot instead.
  2. The round-up engine. Apply the rule to every debit — round up to the nearest ₹100. ₹180 → ₹20, ₹470 → ₹30. Give the user the choice: nearest ₹10 / ₹50 / ₹100, plus a 2x multiplier option.
  3. Actually move the money. Don't pull money every day — collect the week's total (say ₹210/week) and debit it once, via UPI Autopay / e-NACH mandate. Small daily debits will fail and eat charges.
  4. Where the money sits. Don't hold the money yourself — without a licence, that's illegal. Take the partner route: a liquid mutual fund through BSE StAR MF / an AMC / a SEBI-registered distributor, or at the start, a partner bank's savings pot. KYC needs PAN + Aadhaar / DigiLocker.
  5. The app is just 4 screens. Home (how much saved this month), Round-ups list (every payment + its round-up), Goal (e.g. a ₹10,000 emergency fund), Withdraw.

Ready-to-use prompts

What it takes to start

These numbers were not in the reel — they are realistic India estimates.

  • Validation version (no app): ₹0–₹15,000, 1–2 weeks — landing page (Framer / Carrd free), Tally form, Instagram page, domain.
  • Concierge MVP: ₹15,000–₹50,000, 3–4 weeks — 20–50 users, statements processed manually / in a spreadsheet, weekly round-up summary sent to each user.
  • Real app MVP: ₹1.5–₹4 lakh via a freelancer, ₹0 cash if you code it yourself or with a co-founder, 2–3 months — Flutter or React Native, backend, Account Aggregator provider fees, UPI Autopay partner (Razorpay / Cashfree).
  • Skills: One coder, plus one person who understands fintech compliance. Holding funds or giving investment advice needs a SEBI/RBI licence — which is why you start with the partner model.
  • Tools: Figma, Flutter / Next.js, Supabase / Firebase, Finvu or Setu (Account Aggregator), Razorpay (mandate), BSE StAR MF (investment partner).

Launching the first version, step by step

  1. Day 1–3: Put the landing page live — the headline is the same hook, one example card (₹180→₹200), and a waitlist form: name, phone, how many UPI payments you make a day.
  2. Day 4–10: Get 200 waitlist signups from Reels / Shorts. Do 15-minute calls with 20 of them — ask how much they saved last month, and whether they're ready to show their statements.
  3. Week 2–3 — concierge test: Take weekly statements from the 20 ready users, work out the round-ups in a spreadsheet, and WhatsApp each one: "This week you'd save ₹214." Then move that same amount with one UPI request into a separate goal-pot / partner liquid fund in their name. This is the real test — liking a summary is one thing, paying is another.
  4. Week 4: If at least 12 of the 20 pay again in week two, build the app. If retention is under 60%, change the rule first (round up to the nearest ₹50, or debit at a ₹500 threshold instead of weekly).
  5. Month 2–3: Build the app with only the 4 screens above, Android first. Connect the Account Aggregator + weekly Autopay. Give it to the same 20 users first in a Play Store closed test.
  6. Launch day: Don't do a big public launch — open the waitlist in batches of 50, and monitor each user's first successful weekly debit yourself, because in fintech the first failed debit means the user is gone.

The result
