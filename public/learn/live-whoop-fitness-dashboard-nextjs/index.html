I Put My WHOOP Data on My Portfolio: the WHOOP API, Explained by Building a Live Dashboard

How I built /fitness from the WHOOP API: OAuth with rotating refresh tokens, the data model, webhooks, rate limits, the heartbeat intro, and the full prompt.

23 October 2026

My portfolio has a new page: avivashishta.com/fitness. It shows my recovery, last night's sleep, today's strain, thirty days of trends and my last eight workouts, straight from the WHOOP strap on my wrist. It refreshes every hour, and I can't edit the numbers. If I sleep five hours, the page says five hours.

It opens with a heartbeat. A line snaps across the screen, my recovery slams in on the beat, and then both fly into place while the page builds itself around them. The red line keeps moving at my real resting heart rate: at 53 bpm it beats once every 1.13 seconds, because the animation duration is literally 60 / restingHeartRate.

This post is two things: a plain explanation of the WHOOP developer API (what you get, how auth works, where it bites), and how I used it to build the page.

The short version: WHOOP has a free developer API with OAuth 2.0. One server-side function fetches 30 days of recovery, cycles, sleep and workouts (about 10 requests), the page rebuilds at most once an hour, and the only thing I store is the token pair. The hard part isn't the charts. It's that WHOOP refresh tokens rotate, so a serverless app has to keep them somewhere and never refresh twice at once.

The WHOOP API in five minutes

Getting access

  1. Sign in at developer.whoop.com with your WHOOP account and open the Developer Dashboard.
  2. Create a team (you're asked before your first app), then an app. You can have up to five.
  3. Pick your scopes (at least one) and add a redirect URI. The redirect in your OAuth request has to match it exactly.
  4. You get a Client ID and a Client Secret. The secret also signs webhooks, so it never leaves the server.

It's free, and you can start pulling your own data the same day.

The scopes I use

Scope What it unlocks
read:recovery Recovery score, HRV, resting heart rate, SpO2, skin temperature
read:cycles Day strain, calories (as kilojoules), average and max heart rate
read:sleep Stage totals, sleep need, performance, efficiency, consistency, respiratory rate
read:workout Sport, strain, heart rate, distance, minutes in each heart rate zone
read:body_measurement Height, weight, max heart rate
offline A refresh token. Without it your access dies with the first token

Ask only for what you use. People see this list on the consent screen.

The data model

Everything lives under https://api.prod.whoop.com/developer/v2. Four collections and one single record:

Endpoint One record is Joined by
/cycle A WHOOP "day": it starts when you fall asleep and ends when you next fall asleep id
/recovery The morning score for a cycle cycle_id, sleep_id
/activity/sleep One sleep (or nap), with stages id (a UUID in v2)
/activity/workout One activity, with zone durations id (a UUID in v2)
/user/measurement/body Your body measurements none

Five things that will save you an hour each:

  • A WHOOP day isn't a calendar day. Cycles run sleep to sleep, so "one row per day" means keying off the cycle's start time.
  • Every record carries a timezone_offset like +05:30. Times are UTC; shift them yourself to get the wall clock you lived.
  • Scores arrive late. Each record has a score_state, and the score object is missing until it's SCORED. Check it before you read a number.
  • Units are raw. Durations are in milliseconds (total_rem_sleep_time_milli), energy is in kilojoules (divide by 4.184 for kcal), HRV is hrv_rmssd_milli.
  • Naps are sleeps. Sleep records have nap: true|false. Filter them out or your "last night" will be a 20-minute afternoon nap.

Pagination and rate limits

Collections return at most 25 records a page. Pass limit=25 and a start date, and follow the next_token in each response by sending it back as nextToken. Thirty days of everything is about ten requests.

The default limits are 100 requests a minute and 10,000 a day. Every response tells you where you stand in X-RateLimit-Limit, X-RateLimit-Remaining and X-RateLimit-Reset, and going over gets you a 429. With an hourly page you'll never get close.

Webhooks

WHOOP can call a URL when something changes: sleep.updated, recovery.updated, workout.updated and their .deleted twins. New records arrive as updates. The body only names the record (user_id, id, type, trace_id), so you fetch the data yourself. Answer with a 2XX quickly: a failed delivery is retried five times over about an hour.

The terms, in plain words

You can't store copies of the data, feed it to AI models, use it in ads, or use the WHOOP logo. So my page caches each fetch for an hour and stores nothing. It's kept out of my site's AI chat, llms.txt and the Markdown versions that agents read, and it says plainly that it isn't medical advice.

Is it a good idea?

I went back and forth on this, so here's what I weighed.

Why do it. Every portfolio says "I'm consistent". This one proves it with data I don't control. It's also a real integration (OAuth, rate limits, token rotation, caching, webhooks, data viz) that a recruiter or founder can click and see working, which beats a bullet point that says "worked with third-party APIs".

What I'm careful about.

  • It's health data. I'm only publishing my own, and I chose to. Nobody else can connect a strap: the connect link only works with a secret key that lives in my env vars.
  • Routine leaks. Bed and wake times say when I'm home. The page shows last night once it's over and nothing in real time, which I'm fine with. If you build your own, you can drop the times.
  • Bad weeks are public. That's the point. A month of red recovery is honest content too.

How it fits together

WHOOP  ──OAuth──▶  /api/whoop/callback  ──▶  Redis: { access, refresh, expiresAt }
                                               │
/fitness (ISR, revalidate 3600)  ──▶  fetchWhoop(30 days)  ──▶  shapeFitness()  ──▶  page
                                               ▲
WHOOP  ──webhook──▶  /api/whoop/webhook  ──────┘  (throws the cached page away)
  1. A one-time connect. /api/whoop/connect?key=… sends me to WHOOP's consent screen with my scopes plus offline, and an eight-character state (WHOOP wants exactly eight) in an HttpOnly cookie. The callback checks the state, swaps the code for tokens and saves them.
  2. One fetch function. It reads the last 30 days from the four collections in parallel, following next_token.
  3. An hourly page. export const revalidate = 3600 means a thousand visitors or one, WHOOP sees about 10 requests an hour.

Concept 1: refresh tokens that rotate

This is the gotcha. Every time you refresh a WHOOP access token you get a new refresh token, and the old one stops working. On a normal server that's fine. On serverless, two functions can wake up at once, both see an expired token, both refresh, and the second one burns a token that's already dead. Now you're logged out and have to reconnect by hand.

So the tokens live in Redis (Upstash, from the Vercel marketplace), and refreshing takes a lock:

async function accessToken() {
  const saved = await readTokens();
  if (!saved) return null;
  if (saved.expiresAt > Date.now() + 60_000) return saved.access;

  // SET NX: only one caller gets the lock.
  if ((await kv("SET", "whoop:refresh-lock", "1", "NX", "EX", "20")) !== "OK") {
    await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 2000)); // someone else is refreshing
    const after = await readTokens();
    return after && after.expiresAt > Date.now() ? after.access : null;
  }
  try {
    // grant() saves the new pair before returning it
    return (await grant({ grant_type: "refresh_token", refresh_token: saved.refresh, scope: "offline" })).access;
  } finally {
    await kv("DEL", "whoop:refresh-lock");
  }
}

Redis holds two tokens and an expiry time. No health data. If a refresh is refused because I revoked access in the WHOOP app, the tokens get deleted too.

Concept 2: cache the page, never the tokens

In Next.js, a fetch in a server component can be cached along with the page. That's what you want for WHOOP data and exactly what you don't want for the token read: a token cached for an hour has already been rotated away. So every fetch in the WHOOP module is no-store, and the whole loader is wrapped once in unstable_cache with a one-hour lifetime:

export const revalidate = 3600;

const load = unstable_cache(async () => {
  const data = await fetchWhoop(30);
  return data ? shapeFitness(data) : null;
}, ["whoop-fitness"], { revalidate: 3600, tags: ["whoop"] });

The cached thing is the finished dashboard. If WHOOP is down during a rebuild, the loader throws, Next keeps serving the last good page, and nobody sees an error.

Concept 3: turn cycles into days

To build one row per day, I key everything off the cycle's start, shifted to my local clock with the record's own timezone_offset:

function local(iso: string, offset: string) {          // offset like "+05:30"
  const [, sign, hh, mm] = offset.match(/([+-])(\d\d):?(\d\d)/)!;
  return new Date(Date.parse(iso) + (sign === "-" ? -1 : 1) * (+hh * 60 + +mm) * 60_000);
}

The recovery joins the cycle by cycle_id, the sleep joins the recovery by sleep_id, and the result lands on 30 fixed calendar slots. A day without a scored recovery shows up as a gap instead of the chart quietly closing ranks.

Concept 4: compare me to me

A raw HRV of 85 means nothing on its own: everyone's normal is different. So the trend section draws HRV and resting heart rate over a shaded band, my own 30-day average plus or minus one standard deviation. The line turns blue where it's better than my normal and red where it's worse, and for resting heart rate that flips, because lower is better. Each metric gets its own panel and its own scale: two units on one chart with two y-axes always lies to someone.

The rest of the page follows the same rule, one idea per section, and always drawn from the real numbers:

  • Last night: every minute in bed is a dot, grouped by stage (awake, light, deep, REM).
  • Thirty days in a circle: one spoke per day, length is recovery, colour is its zone (blue 67 to 100, yellow 34 to 66, red 0 to 33).
  • Strain, poured: a glass that fills to today's strain out of 21 as you scroll to it.
  • Training: the last eight workouts as a mixing desk, minutes in heart rate zones 1 to 5 on one shared scale.

Every chart answers to the pointer, a tap or the arrow keys, and each has a plain table under it for screen readers.

Concept 5: a webhook so it updates when I wake up

The hourly rebuild means the page could be up to an hour behind. The webhook fixes that: when a sleep, recovery or workout is scored, it throws away the cached dashboard, and the next visit fetches fresh numbers.

Anyone can POST to a public URL, so it checks WHOOP's signature first. WHOOP signs the timestamp header plus the raw body with your client secret:

const expected = createHmac("sha256", process.env.WHOOP_CLIENT_SECRET!)
  .update(timestamp + rawBody)
  .digest();
const given = Buffer.from(req.headers.get("x-whoop-signature")!, "base64");
if (given.length !== expected.length || !timingSafeEqual(given, expected)) return unauthorized();

revalidateTag("whoop", { expire: 0 });
revalidatePath("/fitness");

Read the body with req.text(), not req.json(): the signature covers the exact bytes WHOOP sent. I also reject timestamps older than ten minutes, so a captured request can't be replayed. Now the page usually updates a few minutes after I wake up.

Concept 6: the page loads with a heartbeat

The data is already in the HTML when the page arrives, so the intro isn't a loading screen. It's about a second of staging, once a session:

  1. The hero's own pulse line is lifted above a paper cover, scaled to the full width of the screen, and drawn on.
  2. On the beat, the recovery number slams in top left, huge, from blurred to sharp.
  3. Both glide home, and the rest of the hero cascades in: the zone label wipes in, each card draws its outline, fills, and draws its week with a dot riding the line.

The trick that makes it land cleanly: the giant number and the giant line aren't copies. They're the real hero elements, moved with a transform and then let go, so they end exactly where they live:

// Before the first paint: park the hero's own number big, top left.
number.style.transformOrigin = `${left - p.left}px ${top - p.top}px`;
number.style.transform = `translate(0px, ${t - top}px) scale(${s})`;

// Later: glide home, then hand back to the page's own styles in one frame.
number.animate([{ transform: pose }, { transform: "none" }], { duration: 750, easing: "cubic-bezier(.7,0,.2,1)", fill: "forwards" });

Scrolling, a key, a tap or the skip button sends everything home straight away, and with reduced motion there's no intro at all. Further down, each section's colour wipes in from its own origin as you scroll (night falls from the top, the strain red pours up from the bottom), the wheel grows a day at a time, and the strain glass fills as you reach it.

The prompt

Paste this into Claude Code, Cursor or Replit to build your own. Bring your own WHOOP developer app (free at developer.whoop.com) and an Upstash Redis.

Build a public "live fitness" page for my personal site from my own WHOOP data. Next.js App Router, TypeScript, no chart library (hand-written SVG), no database except Upstash Redis over its REST API for OAuth tokens only.

OAUTH: a GET route /api/whoop/connect that only works when ?key= matches a WHOOP_CONNECT_KEY env var. It redirects to https://api.prod.whoop.com/oauth/oauth2/auth with response_type=code, my client id, redirect_uri = origin + /api/whoop/callback, scope "offline read:recovery read:cycles read:sleep read:workout read:body_measurement", and an 8 character random state saved in an HttpOnly cookie. The callback checks the state, POSTs the code (form-encoded) to https://api.prod.whoop.com/oauth/oauth2/token, saves {access, refresh, expiresAt} to Redis, revalidates the page and redirects to it.

TOKENS ROTATE: every refresh returns a new refresh token and kills the old one. Before each API call, if the access token has under a minute left, take a Redis lock (SET key 1 NX EX 20), refresh, save the new pair, release the lock. If the lock is taken, wait 2 seconds and re-read the tokens instead of refreshing again. If a refresh is refused with 400 or 401, delete the tokens. Every fetch in this module uses cache: "no-store".

DATA: fetch the last 30 days from /developer/v2/recovery, /developer/v2/cycle, /developer/v2/activity/sleep and /developer/v2/activity/workout with limit=25 and start=ISO date, following next_token (sent back as nextToken). Skip any record whose score_state isn't SCORED and drop naps. Map each cycle to a local calendar day with its timezone_offset, attach the recovery (by cycle_id) and the sleep (by the recovery's sleep_id), then fill 30 fixed day slots, leaving missing days empty. Convert milliseconds to minutes or hours and kilojoules to kcal (divide by 4.184).

CACHING: wrap the whole fetch-and-shape in unstable_cache with revalidate 3600 and tag "whoop", and set export const revalidate = 3600 on the page. Never store WHOOP data anywhere else. If the fetch throws at runtime, rethrow so the last good page stays up. In development with no tokens, render a deterministic sample month clearly marked as sample data.

PAGE: a full-screen hero with today's recovery as a huge serif number, its zone (67+ high, 34 to 66 medium, under 34 low) as a small coloured square with a label, a heartbeat line whose red trace sweeps at 60 / resting heart rate seconds per beat, and four cards (HRV, resting HR, hours asleep, strain) each with a 7-day sparkline. Then last night: one dot per minute in bed, grouped by stage. Then a radial wheel, one spoke per day, length = recovery, colour = zone. Then a glass that fills to today's strain out of 21. Then HRV and resting HR over 30 days, each over its own band of average ± one standard deviation, the line blue where better than normal and red where worse, sharing one crosshair driven by pointer and arrow keys. Then the last 8 workouts as bars of minutes in zones 1 to 5 on one shared scale. Then 30-day averages. Every chart gets a table view in a details element.

INTRO: once per session, before first paint, cover the page in paper. Lift the hero's own pulse line and number above the cover with transforms (never copies): draw the line full-width in under half a second, then slam the number in top left, huge, on the beat. After one beat, animate both back to transform: none, fade the cover, and cascade the rest of the hero in with short staggers. Any scroll, key or tap skips to the hand-over. Skip it entirely with prefers-reduced-motion or when the page opens scrolled.

WEBHOOK: a POST route /api/whoop/webhook. Read the raw body with req.text(). Verify the X-WHOOP-Signature header: base64 of HMAC-SHA256 over the X-WHOOP-Signature-Timestamp header value followed by the raw body, keyed with my client secret, compared with timingSafeEqual. Reject timestamps older than ten minutes. On any sleep., recovery. or workout. event, invalidate the cached dashboard and revalidate the page, then return 204.

RULES: no dual-axis charts. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Footnote: "Data from the WHOOP API, read hourly and never stored. Not medical advice." Don't use the WHOOP logo.

Show me the token module first, then the data shaping, then the page.
